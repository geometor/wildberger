Types / person

Content of type 'person'

Apollonius

Author of Conics, whose median theorem and focal conic reflections become derivative-free polynomial laws in W.13, W.20, and W.21.

Archimedes

Hellenistic mathematician after whom Wildberger named Archimedes’ function \(\mathcal{A}(Q_1, Q_2, Q_3)\) for collinearity and triangle quadrea.

Bernoulli

Swiss mathematician who introduced the lemniscate (\(Q = a(1 - 2s)\) in rational polar coordinates, W.28).

Brahmagupta

Indian mathematician whose cyclic quadrilateral area formula (Brahmasphutasiddhanta, 628 CE) underpins cyclic quadrea.

Ceva

Italian geometer who proved Ceva’s concurrency theorem (De lineis rectis, 1678, W.14).

Chebyshev

Russian mathematician whose orthogonal polynomials \(T_n\) rescale onto Wildberger’s spread polynomials \(S_n(s)\) (W.05).

Conway

Introduced Conway triangle notation (\(S_A, S_B, S_C\)), matching the quadratic Cross Law numerators \((Q_j + Q_k - Q_i)/2\).

Desargues

Founder of synthetic projective geometry and harmonic involution theory.

Descartes

Founder of analytic coordinate geometry (La Géométrie, 1637) and discoverer of the folium of Descartes (W.28).

Diocles

Greek geometer whose On Burning Mirrors established the focal reflection property of the parabola.

Euclid

Author of the thirteen books of the Elements, whose synthetic propositions from Books I–IV, VI, and XIII gain field-independent algebraic proofs in Universal Geometry.

Euler

Proved the collinearity of triangle centers along the Euler line (W.16) and the four-point planar distance relation (W.18).

Galileo

Establisher of parabolic projectile kinematics (Two New Sciences, 1638), reformulated via \(S_2(s)\) in W.26.

Hansen

Danish-German astronomer and geodesist who formulated Hansen’s two-station surveying problem (1841, W.25).

Heron

Geometer and engineer whose radical area formula squares cleanly into \(\mathcal{A} = 16 \Delta^2\).

Kepler

Astronomer and geometer whose Golden Right Triangle has pure golden-power quadrances \((1, \phi, \phi^2)\) and spreads \((1/\phi^2, 1/\phi, 1)\) (W.11).

Menelaus

Astronomer and geometer whose transversal theorem shares the exact multiplicative quadrance balance of Ceva’s theorem (W.14).

Minkowski

Formulated relativistic spacetime geometry (1908), corresponding to the Red metric \(Q_R = \Delta x^2 - \Delta y^2\) of Chromogeometry (W.23).

Pacioli

Franciscan mathematician and author of De Divina Proportione (1509, illustrated by Leonardo da Vinci), celebrating \(\phi\) and the five Platonic solids.

Pappus

Author of the Synagoge (Collection), establishing projective cross-ratio invariance (W.22).

Ptolemy

Author of the Almagest, whose cyclic quadrilateral diagonal theorem becomes \(\mathcal{A}(Q_{12}Q_{34}, Q_{23}Q_{14}, Q_{13}Q_{24}) = 0\) (W.18).

Pythagoras

Ancient Greek philosopher-geometer associated with Law I of Rational Trigonometry (\(Q_1 + Q_2 = Q_3 \iff s_3 = 1\)).

Regiomontanus

Renaissance astronomer and mathematician who posed the 1471 extremal viewing-angle problem solved algebraically in W.24.

Seelig

Compiler of the archival notebooks and spreadsheets preserved in geometor/fundamentals.

Snellius

Dutch astronomer and geodesist who pioneered triangulation resection (1615, W.25) and the law of refraction (\(n_1^2 s_1 = n_2^2 s_2\), W.26).

Stewart

Scottish geometer who published Stewart’s cevian theorem (Some General Theorems, 1746, W.13).

Taylor

English mathematician whose polynomial shift expansion defines derivative-free algebraic tangents to conics (W.21).

Wildberger

Canadian-Australian pure mathematician and Associate Professor at the School of Mathematics and Statistics, UNSW Sydney. Creator of Rational Trigonometry and Universal Geometry (Divine Proportions: Rational Trigonometry to Universal Geometry, Wild Egg Books, 2005), which reformulated metric Euclidean and non-Euclidean planar and solid geometry over arbitrary fields without transcendental angles, irrational distances, limits, or trigonometric series. Originator of Chromogeometry (2008), which simultaneously investigates three coupled planar quadratic forms (Blue, Red, Green metrics), and Universal Hyperbolic Geometry (2008–2013).
