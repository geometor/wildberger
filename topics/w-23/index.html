W.23 Chromogeometry & Universal Symmetric Bilinear Forms¶

Harmonizing Blue Euclidean, Red Lorentzian, and Green Hyperbolic Triangle Centers

Replacing the Euclidean quadratic form with any non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form M on F^2 preserves all Five Laws of Rational Trigonometry; in Chromogeometry—pairing Blue (dx^2 + dy^2), Red (dx^2 - dy^2), and Green (2*dx*dy) metrics on the same triangle—the three Euler lines concur at the centroid G, C_B C_R O_B C_G is a parallelogram, and N_B = (C_R + C_G)/2.

A_1 : Point A_2 : Point A_3 : Point ===== t = polygon(A_1, A_2, A_3) G = centroid(A_1, A_2, A_3) C_B, O_B, N_B = centers_blue(A_1, A_2, A_3) C_R, O_R = centers_red(A_1, A_2, A_3) C_G, O_G = centers_green(A_1, A_2, A_3) e_B = line(C_B, O_B) e_R = line(C_R, O_R) e_G = line(C_G, O_G) p_chromo = polygon(C_B, C_R, O_B, C_G)

Mathematical Synthesis¶

Because Rational Trigonometry uses only polynomial identities, every theorem in Parts I–III generalizes to any symmetric bilinear form \(u \cdot_M v = u^T M v\) (\(\det M \neq 0\)) with \(s_M(\ell_1, \ell_2)\) scaled by \(\det M\) (Wildberger2005, Wildberger2008)! In Norman J. Wildberger’s Chromogeometry, three canonical quadratic forms act simultaneously on the same planar triangle t: Blue Euclidean \(Q_B = \Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2\), Red Lorentz/Minkowski \(Q_R = \Delta x^2 - \Delta y^2\), and Green rotated Lorentz \(Q_G = 2 \Delta x \Delta y\), satisfying Pythagoras’ Identity in Metrical Space \(Q_B^2 = Q_R^2 + Q_G^2\)!

Computing the circumcenters (CB, CR, CG), orthocenters (OB, OR, OG), and Euler lines (eB, eR, eG) uncovers miraculous cross-metric harmonies: all three colored Euler lines concur at the metric-free centroid G; the quadrilateral pchromo (\(C_B C_R O_B C_G\)) is always a parallelogram (\(C_B + O_B = C_R + C_G\)); and the Blue nine-point center NB is the exact midpoint of the Red and Green circumcenters!

Key Algebraic Identities¶

\[Q_B = \Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2, \quad Q_R = \Delta x^2 - \Delta y^2, \quad Q_G = 2 \Delta x \Delta y \implies Q_B^2 = Q_R^2 + Q_G^2\]
\[C_B + O_B = C_R + C_G, \qquad N_B = \frac{C_R + C_G}{2}, \qquad N_R = \frac{C_B + C_G}{2}, \qquad N_G = \frac{C_B + C_R}{2}\]
