W.28 Rational Polar Coordinates, Classical Curves & The Ellipson¶

Parametrizing Curves by [s, Q] and Slicing the Cubic Triple-Spread Surface

Replacing polar coordinates (r, theta) with rational polar coordinates [s, Q] where s = x^2 / (x^2 + y^2) (spread to the y-axis) and Q = x^2 + y^2 converts classical polar curves into exact polynomial relations—Bernoulli’s lemniscate s = (Q + 2)/4 (or Q = a*(2*s - 1)), Descartes’ folium (2*s - 1)^2*(s^2 - s + 1)^2*Q^2 - 18*s*(1 - s)*(1 - 3*s + 3*s^2)*Q + 81*s^2*(1 - s)^2 = 0, and the deltoid/ellipson S_3(s) law—while (s_1 + s_2 + s_3)^2 = 2(s_1^2 + s_2^2 + s_3^2) + 4*s_1*s_2*s_3 cuts out the tetrahedral Ellipson.

Mathematical Synthesis¶

Traditional polar coordinates \((r, \theta)\) mix a radical \(r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\) with a transcendental angle \(\theta\), defined only modulo \(2\pi\). Norman J. Wildberger’s rational polar coordinates \([s, Q]\) (Wildberger2005, Chapter 27 & Appendices A–B) pair the polar spread \(s \equiv x^2 / (x^2 + y^2)\) measured from the vertical \(y\)-axis lref (through O and Aref) with the radial quadrance \(Q \equiv x^2 + y^2\), aligning directly with navigation azimuths and 3D rational spherical coordinates.

Classical curves simplify dramatically in \([s, Q]\): Jakob Bernoulli’s figure-eight lemniscate (lemniscate, \((x^2 + y^2)^2 = a(x^2 - y^2)\)) becomes the linear equation \(Q = a(2 s - 1)\) (\(s = (Q + 2)/4\) for \(a = 2\)), while René Descartes’ folium (folium, \(x^3 + y^3 + 3 x y = 0\)) eliminates quadrant signs via \((1 - 3s + 3s^2)^2 - 4 s^3(1 - s)^3 = (2s - 1)^2(s^2 - s + 1)^2\), and the cardioid (cardioid) admits a pure rational parametrization. Crowning the codex is the Ellipson (ellipsonslice): the inflated-tetrahedron cubic surface inside \([0, 1]^3\) defined by the Triple Spread Formula, whose horizontal slices \(s_3 = c\) are ellipses inscribed in \([0, 1]^2\) (an exact circle at \(c = 1/2\)!) and whose oblique slice \(s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 2\) satisfies \(S_3(s) = (36 Q - 5)^2 / (6 Q)^3\)!

Key Algebraic Identities¶

\[s \equiv \frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2}, \quad Q \equiv x^2 + y^2 \implies \text{Lemniscate: } Q = a(2 s - 1), \quad \text{Folium: } (2s - 1)^2(s^2 - s + 1)^2 Q^2 - 18 s(1 - s)(1 - 3s + 3s^2) Q + 81 s^2(1 - s)^2 = 0\]
\[\text{Ellipson }\mathcal{E} \subset [0, 1]^3:\quad (s_1 + s_2 + s_3)^2 = 2(s_1^2 + s_2^2 + s_3^2) + 4 s_1 s_2 s_3 \quad \Big(s_3 = \tfrac{1}{2} \implies \big(s_1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\big)^2 + \big(s_2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\big)^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\Big)\]
