W.03 Quadrance, Midpoints & Point-to-Line Separation¶

Replacing Irrational Euclidean Distance with Exact Quadratic Point Separation

The quadrance between A_1 = [x_1, y_1] and A_2 = [x_2, y_2] is the quadratic form Q(A_1, A_2) = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2; midpoint M divides the quadrance by 4, the locus Q(X, A_1) = Q(X, A_2) is the linear perpendicular bisector, and the quadrance from A_0 to a non-null line l = <a : b : c> is (a*x_0 + b*y_0 + c)^2 / (a^2 + b^2).

A_1 : Point A_2 : Point A_0 : Point ===== l = line(A_1, A_2) M = midpoint(A_1, A_2) b_12 = perp_bisector(A_1, A_2) F_0 = foot(A_0, l)

Mathematical Synthesis¶

Quadrance is the primary point-separation invariant of Rational Trigonometry (Wildberger2005). Because \(Q(A_1, A_2)\) avoids square roots, points in \(F^2\) always have separation in \(F\). Scaling a displacement by \(\lambda\) multiplies its quadrance by \(\lambda^2\), so the midpoint M of A1 and A2 satisfies \(Q(A_1, M) = Q(M, A_2) = Q(A_1, A_2)/4\).

Equating quadrances \(Q(X, A_1) = Q(X, A_2)\) cancels the quadratic terms \(x^2 + y^2\), yielding the linear perpendicular bisector b12 even when \(Q(A_1, A_2)\) is not a square in \(F\)! Similarly, the quadrance from an external point A0 to line l equals \(Q(A_0, F_0)\), and in any parallelogram the diagonal quadrances sum to twice the sum of adjacent side quadrances. Over fields where \(-1 = i^2\) (such as \(\mathbb{F}_{13}\) with \(5^2 \equiv -1\)), non-coincident points along a null line satisfy \(Q(A_1, A_2) = 0\).

Key Algebraic Identities¶

\[Q(A_1, A_2) = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2, \qquad Q(A_1, M) = Q(M, A_2) = \frac{Q(A_1, A_2)}{4}\]
\[Q(A_0, \ell) = \frac{(a x_0 + b y_0 + c)^2}{a^2 + b^2}, \qquad Q_{13} + Q_{24} = 2(Q_{12} + Q_{23})\]
