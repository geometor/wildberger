W.27 3D Planes, De Gua’s Quadrea Law & Platonic Dihedral Spreads¶

Miraculous Cancellation of sqrt(5): Purely Rational Face Spreads 8/9, 1, 8/9, 4/9, 4/5

In 3D space over F, quadrance is Q(A_1, A_2) = dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2, a right-corner tetrahedron satisfies De Gua’s Quadrea Theorem A_base = A_xy + A_yz + A_zx, and the dihedral (face) spreads of ALL FIVE regular Platonic solids—Tetrahedron (8/9), Cube (1), Octahedron (8/9), Icosahedron (4/9), and Dodecahedron (4/5)—are exact rational numbers in Q!

V_0 : Point V_1 : Point V_2 : Point V_3 : Point ===== t_base = polygon(V_1, V_2, V_3) M_edge = midpoint(V_1, V_2) s_ap1 = segment(V_0, M_edge) s_ap2 = segment(V_3, M_edge)

Mathematical Synthesis¶

Extending quadrance to three dimensions (\(\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2 + \Delta z^2\)) defines the dihedral spread \(S(\Pi_1, \Pi_2)\) between two planes via the spread of their normal vectors—equivalently, the spread between face apothems sap1 and sap2 meeting at edge midpoint Medge on base tbase (V0, V1, V2, V3). For a right-corner tetrahedron, Pythagoras’ theorem lifts directly to De Gua’s Quadrea Law: the slant face quadrea equals the sum of the three orthogonal face quadreas!

The crowning 3D revelation of Wildberger2005 (Chapter 26) concerns the five Platonic solids celebrated in Book XIII of Euclid’s Elements and Luca Pacioli’s De Divina Proportione (Pacioli1509). Although the icosahedron and dodecahedron require the pentagon spreads \(\alpha, \beta = (5 \mp \sqrt{5})/8\) solving \(16 s^2 - 20 s + 5 = 0\) (W.19), in the face-spread formulas \(S_{\text{ico}} = \frac{4\beta}{3\alpha}(1 - \frac{\beta}{3\alpha}) = \frac{4}{9}\) and \(S_{\text{dod}} = \frac{4\alpha - 1}{4\alpha^2} = \frac{4}{5}\), every factor of \(\sqrt{5}\) cancels identically, leaving five pure fractions in \(\mathbb{Q}\): \(8/9, 1, 8/9, 4/9, 4/5\)!

Key Algebraic Identities¶

\[16\alpha^2 - 20\alpha + 5 = 0 \implies S_{\text{ico}} = \frac{4\beta}{3\alpha}\!\left(1 - \frac{\beta}{3\alpha}\right) = \frac{4}{9}, \qquad S_{\text{dod}} = \frac{4\alpha - 1}{4\alpha^2} = \frac{4}{5}\]
\[\big(S_{\text{tet}},\; S_{\text{cube}},\; S_{\text{oct}},\; S_{\text{ico}},\; S_{\text{dod}}\big) = \left(\frac{8}{9},\; 1,\; \frac{8}{9},\; \frac{4}{9},\; \frac{4}{5}\right) \in \mathbb{Q}^5\]
